01 · Market signal

What the Agency evidence says

Retail success dominates the conversation, but the operational substrate is the strategic opening: co-manufacturing, supplier control, demand planning, unit economics, packaging compliance, and the systems required to scale without adding chaos.

Commercial territory

Own the gap between winning distribution and operating it profitably. The strongest Logic-specific angle is not “retail consulting”; it is execution across suppliers, inventory, compliance, cost, and channel complexity.

Buyer language

Use concrete operating language: ops nightmare, co-man, bottoms-up forecast, working-capital problem, get back in their queue. Avoid abstract consulting vocabulary.

Interpretation rule: frequency reflects this corpus, not addressable market size. Retail-heavy source composition is a known constraint.
02 · Category expansion

Beyond the food/beverage bias

This supplemental pass expands Agency evidence into beauty operations, electronics hardware, and wine/spirits. It strengthens the manufacturing, sourcing, quality, packaging engineering, freight, compliance, and launch-control layers without mixing unclassified sources into the legacy counts.

Beauty + luxury goods

Compatibility, dispensing, supplier quality, refill systems, luxury presentation, launch timing, and repeat-order economics connect packaging to ongoing operations.

Consumer electronics + babytech

Protective engineering, testing, cube, global sourcing, damage, setup, retailer display, safety, and returns make these high-fit Agency categories.

Wine, spirits + jewelry

Bottle weight, closures, decoration, theft risk, fulfillment, line performance, compliance, and retailer mandates turn presentation into a supply-chain system.

Scaling elevated brands

The shared pattern is earlier decisions, validated specifications, design-for-manufacture, inventory visibility, and one owner across suppliers and channels.

Detailed content briefs

#1 Co-manufacturer selection and scale-up · 94 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, COOs, and operations leaders at scaling beauty, wellness, premium CPG, and beverage brands

Trigger: Missed production windows, inconsistent quality, capacity limits, reformulation, or IP concerns

Core thesis: Co-man selection must test capability, capacity, controls, communication, and commercial fit—not just unit price.

Required elements

Readiness brief; RFQ inputs; capability and capacity screen; audit questions; pilot process; quality agreement; IP/tooling ownership; lead-time validation; scorecard; transition plan

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: Founder evidence on variable lead times and opaque intermediaries; anonymized failure patterns; scorecard; cost-of-delay scenario

Objections to address: We already have a co-man; a broker handles this; audits slow launch; overseas is always cheaper

Soft CTA: Use the scorecard to pressure-test your current or shortlisted co-man.

#2 Demand planning inventory and working capital · 92 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, COOs, finance leads, and demand planners at omnichannel brands

Trigger: Stockouts alongside excess inventory, wholesale launch, long lead times, or cash trapped in slow SKUs

Core thesis: Forecasting is a cross-functional operating cadence that protects service levels and cash together.

Required elements

Channel inputs; bottoms-up forecast; SKU segmentation; lead-time/MOQ assumptions; safety stock; launch scenarios; open-to-buy; accuracy and bias; operating cadence; ownership map

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: SEEQ and Dossier founder quotes; over-order scenario; cash-conversion example; downloadable model

Objections to address: Sales is unpredictable; we are too early for S&OP; software solves it; more inventory is safer

Soft CTA: Model one high-risk SKU before the next purchase order.

#3 Gross margin landed cost and cost-to-serve · 89 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, CFOs, and COOs adding wholesale, retail, international, or complex fulfillment

Trigger: Revenue rises while cash and contribution margin weaken

Core thesis: Gross margin hides the operational leakage that determines whether growth creates or consumes cash.

Required elements

Landed-cost waterfall; duties/freight; packaging/assembly; 3PL; deductions; returns/damage; trade spend; channel contribution; SKU/customer profitability; review cadence

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: Channel comparison; packaging-cube example; anonymized cost waterfall; authorized case metrics where relevant

Objections to address: Our gross margin is healthy; finance tracks this; freight is temporary; volume offsets leakage

Soft CTA: Run one hero SKU through the full cost-to-serve waterfall.

#4 Supplier resilience contracts and quality control · 87 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, COOs, sourcing leaders, and quality leaders in beauty, wellness, hardware, and spirits

Trigger: Single-source dependency, recurring defects, certification surprises, or communication failures

Core thesis: Resilience is designed through specifications, contracts, testing, alternates, and escalation rules before failure.

Required elements

Dependency map; critical-component risk; approved specs; quality agreement; inspection/testing; change notification; certifications; capacity health; secondary sourcing; corrective actions; escalation tree

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: Hardware supplier-dependency quote; beauty compatibility examples; defect-cost scenario; risk matrix

Objections to address: A second source costs more; the relationship is strong; contracts cannot prevent defects; quality systems are overhead

Soft CTA: Map the three supplier failures that could stop revenue this quarter.

#5 Operational readiness for retail expansion · 85 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, COOs, sales leaders, and operations teams preparing for major retail or distribution

Trigger: Authorization arrives before systems and cash are ready

Core thesis: Getting on shelf is a commercial win; staying there requires inventory, compliance, data, fulfillment, and cash readiness before the first PO.

Required elements

Readiness gates; unit economics; forecast/PO plan; onboarding; EDI/ASNs; routing guides; packaging/labeling; deductions; distributor terms; promotional inventory; velocity review; 30/60/90 plan

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: Retail-buyer and founder quotes; checklist; first-PO cash scenario; beauty, hardware, and beverage examples

Objections to address: The broker handles it; retailer forecasts are enough; compliance can wait; we will fix issues after launch

Soft CTA: Pressure-test the operating plan before accepting the launch date.

#6 3PL selection fulfillment and returns · 82 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, COOs, and ecommerce or operations leaders outgrowing a warehouse or incumbent 3PL

Trigger: Weak visibility, rising errors or damage, retail requirements, kitting, or poor returns handling

Core thesis: A 3PL should be selected against the brand's operating model and next channel—not a generic rate card.

Required elements

Order/SKU profile; B2B versus DTC; WMS/integrations; SLAs; accuracy; kitting; lot/expiry control; returns; retail compliance; peak capacity; implementation; exit terms; RFP scorecard

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: Dossier specialization evidence; damage/returns examples; weighted RFP; total-cost comparison

Objections to address: All 3PLs are similar; cheapest pick fee wins; Shopify integration is enough; switching is too risky

Soft CTA: Use the requirements matrix before requesting another 3PL quote.

#7 Beauty and personal-care operations · 80 points

ICP and buying moment

Trigger: SKU proliferation, retail expansion, formula-pack incompatibility, claims pressure, or component lead times

Core thesis: Beauty operations must coordinate formula, components, claims, suppliers, testing, inventory, and launch timing as one system.

Soft CTA: Map formula, pack, claims, and inventory dependencies on one launch calendar.

#8 Consumer hardware and babytech operations · 77 points

Trigger: Long commitments, certification risk, supplier concentration, retail launch, or high returns

#9 Packaging engineering as an operations lever · 75 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, COOs, sourcing, packaging, and logistics leaders across premium products

Trigger: Packaging cost rises, damage persists, freight cube is high, or requirements change

Core thesis: Packaging engineering changes margin, freight, quality, throughput, compliance, and experience simultaneously.

Required elements

System cost versus unit price; dimensions/cube; structure; line compatibility; pack-out labor; damage testing; palletization; retail compliance; sustainability; specification control; transition

Evidence and persuasion

Objections to address: Redesign risks the brand; savings are too small; sustainability costs more; supplier owns specs

Soft CTA: Evaluate one package on total system cost, not piece price.

#10 Embedded operations versus internal hiring · 72 points

ICP and buying moment

Audience: Founders, CEOs, investors, and finance leaders deciding how to add operating capacity

Trigger: Founder remains the escalation point, hiring is slow, and problems span several specialties

Core thesis: The choice is which operating model provides accountable execution at the brand's current complexity and risk.

Required elements

Readiness signals; ownership scope; hire cost/time; fractional versus project versus embedded; specialties; governance; transition; 90-day outcomes; when to hire internally

Evidence and persuasion

Proof to include: Current economics only after confirmation; workload scenarios; responsibility matrix; founder-capacity evidence; authorized outcomes

Objections to address: Outside teams lack context; we need one owner; retainers get expensive; wait until after the raise

Soft CTA: Compare the next 12 months of operating needs—not just one salary line.
